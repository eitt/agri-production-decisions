\title{Agricultural Production Planning under Correlated Price Risk: Exact Reference Computation and Surrogate-Guided Continuous Refinement}

\author{\name{Leonardo Hern\'an Talero-Sarmiento}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agricultural production planning couples discrete crop calendars with continuous land allocation under uncertain output prices. This study formulates a bi-objective mixed-integer planning model that maximizes expected income and minimizes covariance-based price risk while enforcing empirical crop eligibility, lot occupancy, maturation, and botanical-family recovery. Four publication-complete instances cover one or two farms, 2--8 lots, 5--12 crops, 52--104 planning weeks, and 0.6--6.0 ha. Exact $\varepsilon$-constraint runs in SCIP provide an incumbent reference, standard NSGA-II supplies a low-cost baseline, and two matheuristics apply either full or surrogate-guided continuous quadratic area refinement with SLSQP. Each stochastic method is evaluated over ten seeds using normalized hypervolume, IGD$^+$, additive epsilon, nondominated-set size, runtime, and refinement effort. Exact-run time rises from 51.25 s on the two-lot instance to 9.52 h on the largest completed instance, while most intermediate SCIP points on the larger instances terminate at the time limit. Surrogate gating reduces refinement calls from 12,000 to 3,000 per replication. On the 8-lot, 104-week instance, it reduces mean total runtime from 1,478.48 to 410.52 s relative to full refinement, but median hypervolume also declines from 0.745 to 0.718. Standard NSGA-II records the highest median hypervolume among the stochastic methods on three of four instances. The evidence therefore supports method selection as a trade-off among reference quality, computation, and planning scale, rather than a universal algorithm ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Production planning must frequently commit heterogeneous resources before uncertain economic conditions become observable, while technological or biological lead times make many assignments expensive to reverse. Agricultural planning presents this challenge in a particularly transparent form because land, planting windows, maturation periods, recovery requirements, and correlated output prices jointly determine which production sequences remain feasible and economically defensible \citep{Ahumada2009,Tan2012}.

The resulting planning problem cannot be reduced to continuous resource utilization, because deliberately unoccupied capacity can emerge for fundamentally different operational reasons. Some inactivity is mandatory when calendars, maturation, setup, crop-family recovery, or horizon boundaries prevent production, whereas other inactivity is endogenous when a planner postpones an event, leaves a slot unused, or reduces cultivated area to improve the expected-income--risk compromise. Production research has long shown that zero-production periods and inserted idle time can be economically or operationally rational rather than automatically inefficient \citep{WagnerWhitin1958,KanetSridharan2000,Jodlbauer2006,TrevinoMartinez2022}.

Agricultural models nevertheless add empirical and structural complications that classical lot-sizing or machine-scheduling abstractions do not represent directly. Yield evidence varies geographically, crop eligibility depends on observed municipality--crop support, harvest timing follows crop-specific maturation, and crop prices move jointly across markets. Existing studies address uncertain maturation, demand, harvest, and yield, while portfolio formulations connect crop diversification with financial or environmental exposure \citep{Tan2012,Radulescu2014,Burbano2022}; however, implementable weekly plans must also coordinate discrete event sequences with continuous cultivated areas.

This mixed discrete--continuous structure creates a consequential methodological choice between exact optimization, evolutionary exploration, and matheuristic refinement. Exact multiobjective procedures offer status-bearing incumbent trade-offs, population-based search explores extensive nonconvex schedule spaces, and conditional mathematical refinement can improve continuous allocations after discrete crop identities and dates have been decoded \citep{Mavrotas2009,Deb2002,Hu2024}. Refining every offspring can nevertheless become computationally dominant, which motivates surrogate-guided allocation of optimization effort rather than surrogate replacement of the economic objectives \citep{Jin2011,Liu2024}.

Accordingly, this study asks three questions connecting production-planning value, computational scale, and selective mathematical improvement. RQ1 examines how rapidly exact $\varepsilon$-constraint reference computation becomes costly as farms, lots, crops, planning weeks, and feasible events increase jointly; RQ2 compares standard NSGA-II with exhaustive and selective refinement against status-aware SCIP incumbent archives; and RQ3 evaluates whether surrogate-guided selection reduces refinement effort while retaining defensible Pareto quality under constrained runtime budgets.

The study contributes a weekly bi-objective production-planning model that maximizes expected income and minimizes covariance-based monetary price risk under empirical eligibility and agronomic feasibility, together with a controlled comparison of four computational roles. The experiment reports certification status, Pareto quality, runtime, refinement effort, and ex-post operational diagnostics without treating utilization as an optimization objective, without equating every inactive period with inefficiency, and without extending agricultural evidence into unsupported cross-industry empirical claims.

\section{Related Research}

\subsection{Production planning under uncertainty and non-continuous resource use}

Production planning under uncertainty requires explicit trade-offs among economic performance, exposure, timing, and heterogeneous resource availability, because a single utilization measure cannot distinguish productive discipline from strategically preserved flexibility. Reviews of modern planning problems document expanding operational constraints and solution families, while hybridization research shows why structured problems increasingly combine mathematical programming with metaheuristic exploration \citep{Battaia2013,Boysen2022,Battaia2022}.

Classical production research also establishes that inactivity cannot be interpreted uniformly as waste. The Wagner--Whitin lot-sizing model admits zero-production periods when setup and inventory economics favour temporally concentrated production \citep{WagnerWhitin1958}, while the scheduling taxonomy of \citet{KanetSridharan2000} identifies inserted idle time as a purposeful decision under several performance criteria. Integrated lot-sizing and scheduling further connects production quantities, setups, and sequencing decisions \citep{Jodlbauer2006}, demonstrating a structural principle that transfers across sectors even when its empirical magnitude remains application-specific.

Contemporary energy-aware scheduling reinforces that distinction because planned idle time can reduce energy cost or emissions when tariffs and job sequences vary temporally. \citet{TrevinoMartinez2022} explicitly show that strategically planned idle time need not constitute waste, while maintenance-integrated scheduling uses inserted inactivity to exploit time-of-use tariffs \citep{An2023}. These studies support a cautious conceptual claim that inactivity may be purposeful, rather than an empirical claim that manufacturing results automatically generalize to agriculture.

Agricultural production planning combines that general structural distinction with biological timing, uncertain market outcomes, spatially variable productivity, and operational decisions extending from planting through harvest. The agri-food review by \citet{Ahumada2009} connects planning across these decisions, while \citet{Tan2012} represent demand, maturation, harvest, and yield risk for annual crops. Mandatory inactivity therefore arises whenever sowing calendars, maturation, family recovery, or horizon feasibility preclude another event, whereas endogenous inactivity arises from decoded waiting choices, unused event slots, and reduced cultivated areas.

Portfolio formulations provide a complementary economic perspective because crop returns are jointly distributed, meaning diversification depends on covariance rather than independent crop-specific penalties. \citet{Radulescu2014} integrate expected return with financial and environmental risks, whereas \citet{Burbano2022} apply decision analysis and portfolio theory to horticultural diversification. The present setting connects that covariance-aware perspective with weekly lot occupation, continuous cultivated area, and empirical municipality--crop eligibility, while deliberately excluding weather, yield, behavioural, and complete supply-chain uncertainty.

\subsection{Agricultural multiobjective planning and matheuristic solution paradigms}

Agricultural multiobjective studies increasingly connect economic performance with environmental exposure, distribution, sustainability, and interactive decision support while preserving application-specific agronomic restrictions. Recent Colombian cultivation research integrates production and distribution decisions \citep{Vergara2025}, enhanced NSGA-II designs incorporate fuzzy information and expert rules \citep{Huang2025}, and interactive evolutionary optimization reduces evaluation demands while supporting agricultural trade-off exploration \citep{Kropp2026}.

Matheuristics exploit complementary strengths by assigning combinatorial exploration to a heuristic while mathematical programming resolves a structured subproblem or improves selected candidate solutions. In multiobjective cellular manufacturing, \citet{Hu2024} embed mathematical optimization within evolutionary scheduling, showing how exact structure can support rather than replace population-based search. This decomposition is especially relevant when fixing discrete identities and times converts the remaining allocation problem into a more tractable continuous program.

Production applications demonstrate several ways to connect mathematical and heuristic components, including formulation-driven neighborhoods, decomposition, route improvement, and coordinated scheduling decisions. \citet{Li2022} combine an efficient formulation with a matheuristic for unrelated parallel machines, while \citet{Qin2024} address capacitated multi-level lot sizing with substitution and backorders. Related designs improve production routing \citep{Vadseth2023} and integrate production with distribution scheduling \citep{Vie2025}, confirming that hybrid structure can broaden computational reach across planning levels.

Industrial evidence also indicates that practical matheuristics must be judged through computational reliability and decision quality under realistic planning horizons rather than through algorithm labels alone. \citet{Seyfi2026} integrate lot sizing, machine scheduling, shifts, and workforce planning for washing-machine production, obtaining scalable feasible solutions when longer horizons challenge direct optimization. These studies motivate conditional mathematical refinement, but they rarely isolate whether refining every evolutionary candidate is preferable to allocating refinement through learned utility.

\subsection{Surrogate-guided optimization and research gap}

Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation replaces or prioritizes expensive evaluations using models trained on previously evaluated candidates, thereby treating computation as a resource-allocation decision. Early synthesis emphasizes that imperfect surrogates require explicit model management and continued contact with true evaluations to avoid misleading the search \citep{Jin2011}. Multiobjective surveys similarly identify accuracy, dimensionality, infill selection, and limited evaluation budgets as central design challenges for computationally expensive optimization \citep{Chugh2019}.

More recent reviews broaden this foundation by classifying surrogate models, algorithmic roles, applications, and unresolved questions across continuous and combinatorial expensive optimization. \citet{He2023} review surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms across several problem classes and applications, while \citet{Liu2024} show that expensive combinatorial settings remain substantially less developed than continuous counterparts. A recent differential-evolution review likewise stresses that real-world value depends on whether surrogate overhead remains small relative to the evaluations being rationed \citep{Yu2025}.

Current algorithmic developments improve relationship learning and local-region management for expensive multiobjective problems, but their customary goal remains prediction or replacement of expensive objective evaluations. \citet{Pierce2025} learn informative comparison relationships among candidate solutions, whereas \citet{Wang2026} adapt local-region search for challenging high-dimensional expensive multiobjective optimization problems within demanding practical settings. The present study instead predicts realized optimization gain and uses that estimate only to decide which candidates receive a true continuous-refinement call.

Across these linked streams, no reviewed study jointly provides weekly agricultural scheduling, covariance-based monetary risk, exact status-aware references, exhaustive continuous refinement, and surrogate-gated refinement within one controlled experiment. This gap supports a focused contribution concerning the allocation of optimization effort, while avoiding broader claims that the underlying algorithms or agricultural constraints are individually unprecedented.

\section{Materials and Data}

\subsection{Planning setting and productivity evidence}

The decision setting represents Colombian farms divided into heterogeneous lots. A production event assigns a crop, sowing week, harvest week, and cultivated area to one lot. The source productivity table contains 17,059 municipality--crop--planting-month observations, 17 municipalities, and 40 recorded agricultural products. These counts describe the source table; the completed benchmarks use supported subsets of 5--12 crops rather than all 40 products.

Yield estimation combines municipality, crop, and planting-period evidence. Crop eligibility requires at least three supporting observations for a lot's municipality--crop pair; if no crop passes that threshold for a generated lot, the crop with greatest support is retained to avoid a structurally unusable lot. Feasible event variables are created only when the sowing calendar permits the event, harvest occurs within the horizon, the empirical eligibility rule is satisfied, and expected yield is positive.

\subsection{Market prices, forecasting, and uncertainty representation}

Weekly prices were constructed for Bucaramanga's Centroabastos market from DANE SIPSA wholesale workbooks for 2018--2026. The input provenance records the downloaded files and hashes. The experiment uses a 104-week price forecast generated from the processed weekly series; shorter instances use its first 52 weeks. A Ledoit--Wolf covariance estimate of crop log returns supplies a positive-semidefinite dependence matrix, with numerical symmetrization and a diagonal adjustment only if required for convexity.

The optimization is performed in Colombian pesos (COP). Publication tables and figures apply a frozen reporting rate of 3,306.86 COP/USD dated 28 September 2026. If $p_{kt}$ is COP/kg, then $p^{\mathrm{USD}}_{kt}=p_{kt}/3306.86$. Likewise, income in million COP is converted to thousand USD by multiplication by $1000/3306.86$, and risk based on million-COP exposure is converted by $(1000/3306.86)^2$. Positive constant rescaling leaves Pareto dominance unchanged; the quadratic risk unit changes by the square of the monetary conversion factor.

No \texttt{demand\_weekly.csv} file was present in the reproducible computational snapshot. Consequently, the reported experiment has no market-throughput cap and makes no use of SIPSA-A demand.

\subsection{Benchmark construction and empirical preprocessing}

The benchmark ladder changes farms, lots, area, crop count, and horizon under fixed instance seeds. Table~\ref{tab:instances} reports the publication-complete portion of this ladder and preserves the configured progression from small single-farm cases toward larger heterogeneous planning settings.

Only instances with all expected files were admitted: one exact run and ten seeds (2026--2035) for each stochastic method. \section{Mathematical Formulation}

The decision problem selects agronomically feasible crop events and their areas. Expected income is maximized while correlated price risk is minimized. The formulation is expressed in the internal monetary units used by optimization; the ex-post USD transformation above is used for manuscript reporting.

\subsection{Sets, parameters, and variables}

Let $\Fset$ be the farms, $\Lset$ the lots, and $\Lset_f\subseteq\Lset$ the lots belonging to farm $f$. Let $\Kset$ denote crops, $\Tset$ weekly planning periods, and $\Tset_k\subseteq\Tset$ feasible harvest periods for crop $k$. For an optional market extension, $\Gset$ denotes sales groups. The sets $\mathcal{C}_{lw}$ and $\mathcal{C}^{q}_{lw}$ contain, respectively, crop--harvest events that occupy lot $l$ during week $w$ and events from botanical family $q$ whose occupation or recovery intervals conflict in that week.

Parameter $A_l$ is lot area (m$^2$); $n_k$ is crop maturation duration (weeks); $s(k,t)=t-n_k$ is the sowing week implied by harvest $t$; $r_{lks}$ is expected yield (kg/m$^2$); and $p_{kt}$ is expected price (COP/kg). The indicator $b_{lk}$ equals one when empirical evidence makes crop $k$ eligible for lot $l$. Matrix $\Sigma\succeq0$ is the covariance matrix of crop price log returns, $q(k)$ is botanical family, and $g(k)$ is sales group. The optional parameter $D_{gt}$ would cap group-$g$ throughput in week $t$, but is absent from the reported base experiment.

For every feasible event $(l,k,t)$, define
\begin{equation}
y_{lkt}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if crop }k\text{ is activated on lot }l\text{ for harvest in week }t,\\0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and let $z_{lkt}\geq0$ be cultivated area (m$^2$). Aggregate harvested quantity is $x_{kt}\geq0$ (kg). Monetary exposure is $e_{kt}\geq0$, measured in million COP, and $\rho\geq0$ is the epigraph variable used for quadratic risk in the exact formulation.

\subsection{Bi-objective production-planning model}

Production and exposure are aggregated through
\begin{align}
x_{kt} &= \sum_{l\in\Lset} r_{lk,s(k,t)}z_{lkt}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset_k, \label{eq:production}\\
e_{kt} &= \frac{p_{kt}x_{kt}}{10^6}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset. \label{eq:exposure}
\end{align}
The two objectives are
\begin{align}
\max\quad F_1 &= \sum_{k\in\Kset}\sum_{t\in\Tset}e_{kt}, \label{eq:income}\\
\min\quad F_2 &= \sum_{t\in\Tset}\mathbf e_t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf e_t, \label{eq:risk}
\end{align}
where $F_1$ is expected income in million COP and $F_2$ is correlated price risk in (million COP)$^2$. The latter is a monetary mean--variance measure, not a production cost.

Neither objective rewards continuous land occupation, so reduced productive intensity can become an endogenous component of the expected-income--risk compromise rather than evidence of algorithmic failure. The feasible zero-production schedule has zero monetary price exposure, while intermediate Pareto solutions can reduce exposure through smaller cultivated areas, fewer events, or longer discretionary intervals; operational use is therefore evaluated only after optimization.

Activation uses the physical lot area rather than an arbitrary large constant:
\begin{equation}
0\le z_{lkt}\le A_l y_{lkt}, \qquad l\in\Lset,\ k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset_k. \label{eq:link}
\end{equation}
Weekly conflict cliques enforce exclusive lot occupation,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{lw}}y_{lkt}\le1,
\qquad l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset, \label{eq:occupancy}
\end{equation}
and family-specific cliques extend this restriction through the configured recovery interval,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal{C}^{q}_{lw}}y_{lkt}\le1,
\qquad l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset,\ q. \label{eq:rotation}
\end{equation}
Variables exist only if $b_{lk}=1$, $s(k,t)$ is an allowed sowing week, $t$ lies within the horizon, and $r_{lk,s(k,t)}>0$. This event generation enforces empirical eligibility and temporal feasibility without adding redundant zero variables.

\noindent\textit{Optional market-throughput extension.}
If independent market-capacity evidence is available, the implementation can add
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k:g(k)=g}x_{kt}\le D_{gt},\qquad g\in\Gset,\ t\in\Tset. \label{eq:optional-demand}
\end{equation}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:optional-demand} was inactive in every result reported here.

\subsection{Epsilon-constraint reference formulation}

The reference procedure first solves
\begin{equation}
I^{\max}=\max\{F_1:(\ref{eq:production})--(\ref{eq:rotation})\}. \label{eq:maxincome}
\end{equation}
For each configured $\eta\in\{0,0.1,\ldots,1\}$, SCIP then solves
\begin{align}
\min\quad &\rho,\nonumber\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\rho\ge F_2, \label{eq:epigraph}\\
&F_1\ge\eta I^{\max}, \label{eq:epsilon}\
&(\ref{eq:production})--(\ref{eq:rotation}).\nonumber
\end{align}
When a feasible incumbent is found, an optional strong-Pareto tie break maximizes $F_1$ subject to the attained risk plus a numerical tolerance. Solver status and relative gap are retained for every point, so a feasible time-limited incumbent is not relabelled as certified optimal.

The decoder enforces empirical eligibility, temporal availability, botanical-family recovery, horizon feasibility, and positive output before evaluating income and risk. Mandatory inactivity arises from sowing calendars, maturation, setup, family recovery, and horizon truncation, whereas endogenous inactivity arises from unused slots, waiting genes permitting zero to four additional weeks, and reduced cultivated-area fractions. This distinction prevents every unoccupied interval from being interpreted automatically as scheduling inefficiency.

\subsection{Common NSGA-II backbone and continuous refinement}

Binary tournament selection, crossover probability 0.90, mutation, nondominated sorting, and crowding-based environmental selection operate with population 80 for 150 generations. Ten fixed seeds permit stochastic replication without changing any instance data, while every candidate retains true decoded or mathematically refined economic objective values.

\noindent\textit{Baseline NSGA-II.}
Standard NSGA-II retains the decoded cultivated areas and objective values without invoking continuous mathematical refinement, thereby providing the common low-cost evolutionary reference for both hybrid variants.

\noindent\textit{Full refinement.}

Conditional on a decoded discrete schedule, area allocation has a linear income expression, a positive-semidefinite quadratic risk expression, physical lot constraints, and fixed event timing. The reported NSGA-II+refinement experiment sends every offspring to this continuous subproblem. Although its mathematical structure is a convex quadratic refinement, the computational backend in this experiment is SLSQP, not SCIP.

\subsection{Surrogate-guided refinement}

H-NSGA-II uses the same evolutionary operators and SLSQP refinement, but limits refinement to 25\% of offspring. During five warm-up generations, candidates are sampled for refinement. After at least 30 realized gains are available, an Extra Trees regressor ranks candidates; 20\% of each refinement budget remains random exploration. All selection, nondominated sorting, and reported objectives use actual decoded or SLSQP-refined values. Surrogate predictions only control access to refinement.

Figure~\ref{fig:method_variants} isolates the principal algorithmic contrast by holding evolutionary generation and Pareto survival constant while changing only how conditional area refinement is allocated. The surrogate estimates realized refinement gain for candidate prioritization, whereas every selection decision and archived objective value remains grounded in decoded or SLSQP-refined evaluations.

\subsection{Controlled computational design}

All results come from \texttt{results/publication\_slsqp\_test}. Complete instances contain one exact run and 30 stochastic runs: ten each for NSGA-II, full refinement, and H-NSGA-II. Incomplete instances are excluded as units; a finished seed cannot enter tables unless every required seed and method for that instance is present. Optimization outputs remain unchanged, and manuscript currency conversion is reporting-only.

SCIP solves the mixed-integer quadratically constrained formulation at 11 income thresholds for I01--I04, using four threads and a target relative gap of $10^{-4}$. Instance-specific limits are 300, 600, 1,200, and 1,800 s per solve, while the optional strong-Pareto tie break can require a second solve and therefore raise total runtime beyond 11 times the nominal point limit.

\subsection{Pareto, computational, and operational performance indicators}

For I01--I04, the nondominated SCIP incumbent archive defines the normalization and reference set. This label is deliberate because time-limited points may have nonzero or uninformative gaps. Hypervolume uses reference point $(1.1,1.1)$ after objective normalization; higher values indicate greater dominated normalized area. Lower IGD$^+$ and additive epsilon are preferred.

Nondominated point count, total runtime, refinement calls, cumulative refinement time, and useful-refinement hit rate complement these quality measures. Medians summarize stochastic quality and runtime, while mean runtime is used only for explicitly stated runtime ratios.

Operational diagnostics are calculated ex post from decoded schedules and are neither optimization objectives nor binding constraints. For a decoded event $e$ with cultivated area $a_e$, sowing week $s_e$, and harvest week $h_e$, area-adjusted space--time utilization is
\begin{equation}
U=\frac{\sum_e a_e(h_e-s_e)}{|\Tset|\sum_{l\in\Lset}A_l}. \label{eq:utilization}
\end{equation}
Lot temporal occupancy is $U_l^{\mathrm{time}}=|\cup_{e\in\mathcal E_l}[s_e,h_e)|/|\Tset|$, while mean idle weeks averages the horizon weeks outside those occupied unions across lots. Together, these post-hoc measures distinguish partial-area intensity from temporal occupation, although they cannot by themselves classify every inactive interval as mandatory or economically chosen.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Exact computation and scalability}

Total exact-run time increases from 51.25 s for I01 to 9,185.10 s (2.55 h) for I02, 22,824.80 s (6.34 h) for I03, and 34,274.58 s (9.52 h) for I04. Status evidence changes with scale. Ten of 11 I01 points are optimal and one terminates at the target gap. I02 has four optimal and seven time-limited points. I03 and I04 each have one optimal income-endpoint and ten time-limited points. Thus, SCIP remains valuable as an incumbent reference, but the experiment does not establish full-front optimality beyond the smallest instance.

\subsection{Refinement efficiency and decision interpretation}

Full refinement makes 12,000 SLSQP calls per replication; surrogate gating makes 3,000, a 75\% reduction. On I03, median cumulative refinement time falls from 1,346.04 to 284.46 s and useful-refinement hit rate rises from 96.0\% to 98.8\%. On I04, refinement time falls from 289.67 to 71.54 s and hit rate rises from 94.8\% to 98.9\%. The higher hit rate shows concentrated allocation, but quality must be read jointly: median hypervolume falls from 0.745 to 0.718 on I03 and from 0.829 to 0.793 on I04. Surrogate gating is therefore a computational economy with a measured quality cost in these instances.

\section{Discussion}

The algorithmic comparison separates three computational roles under identical benchmark data, chromosome semantics, evolutionary operators, and stochastic seeds. SCIP supplies economically interpretable incumbent references and explicit status information, but its rapid runtime growth and time-limited points restrict certification beyond I01; standard NSGA-II supplies comparatively inexpensive diverse fronts; and continuous refinement exploits the conditional area-allocation structure without necessarily improving the global archive. Surrogate gating directs one quarter as many SLSQP calls toward candidates with higher useful-refinement rates and substantially reduces runtime on larger completed instances, although lower median hypervolume shows that omitted calls can still influence final-front quality.

The production schedules also demonstrate why inactivity should not be interpreted automatically as inefficiency. Agronomic calendars, maturation, setup, botanical-family recovery, and horizon boundaries create mandatory gaps, whereas waiting genes, unused slots, and cultivated-area reductions create endogenous gaps that may alter harvest prices or monetary exposure. Because utilization is neither an objective nor a constraint, the reported space--time utilization, lot temporal occupancy, and mean idle weeks remain post-hoc diagnostics whose interpretation requires the decoded schedule and cannot be reduced to a universal efficiency judgment.

The cross-industry literature supports a structural, rather than empirical, transferability claim. Lot-sizing models, inserted-idle-time scheduling, integrated production planning, and energy-aware scheduling all show that zero production or planned inactivity can be rational under specific costs, constraints, and temporal incentives \citep{WagnerWhitin1958,KanetSridharan2000,Jodlbauer2006,TrevinoMartinez2022,An2023}. Agricultural production differs in its biological calendars, spatial yield evidence, and crop-family recovery, so those manufacturing findings justify distinguishing forms of inactivity without establishing that their estimated effects or preferred policies apply directly to farms.

Several limitations bound inference from the controlled experiment. Four complete instances and ten stochastic seeds per method cannot support universal algorithm rankings, while time-limited SCIP points mean that quality indicators measure agreement with incumbent archives rather than distance to proven Pareto sets. The covariance objective captures correlated price exposure but excludes weather, yield, behavioural, and demand uncertainty; the optional market-throughput constraint remained inactive because no reproducible demand file was available; and uneven municipality--crop evidence limits empirical coverage. Future work should compare equal wall-clock budgets, vary surrogate allocation rules, complete larger benchmark ladders, and test explicitly modelled production-intensity requirements before claiming robustness under stronger utilization expectations.

\section{Conclusion}

This study formulates weekly agricultural production planning as a bi-objective event model combining discrete crop calendars, continuous cultivated area, empirical crop eligibility, and covariance-based monetary price risk. The formulation preserves expected income maximization and correlated price-risk minimization as its only objectives, while operational utilization remains an explicitly post-hoc diagnostic rather than an optimization target.

The computational evidence reveals a three-way trade-off among certification, Pareto quality, and response time. Exact $\varepsilon$-constraint computation provides status-bearing incumbent references but grows from seconds to hours and loses full-front certification as scale increases, whereas standard NSGA-II remains the lowest-cost stochastic method and achieves the highest median hypervolume among stochastic alternatives on three instances. Surrogate gating reduces refinement calls by 75\% and substantially lowers refinement and total runtime on the larger completed instances, although these savings coincide with lower median Pareto quality than full refinement.

The operational interpretation is equally consequential because unoccupied time combines mandatory agronomic restrictions with endogenous economic choices, and therefore cannot be labelled inefficient without examining the decoded schedule. Larger benchmark conclusions require completion of every prescribed seed under the same snapshot rules, while future research should test stronger production-intensity requirements, richer uncertainty sources, alternative surrogate budgets, and equal-time computational comparisons before extending the present findings beyond the evaluated agricultural instances.
