\begin{abstract}
Production planning becomes difficult when discrete sequencing, continuous resource allocation, biological lead times, and uncertain economic conditions interact. Agricultural systems make this coupling explicit because planting calendars, maturation, land occupation, and correlated selling prices jointly shape feasible and economically defensible plans. Exact and evolutionary approaches offer complementary strengths, yet it remains unclear when continuous mathematical refinement should be applied exhaustively and when its cost justifies selective allocation. We address this gap through a bi-objective event model that maximizes expected gross revenue and minimizes contemporaneous covariance-based price risk, evaluated with SCIP $\varepsilon$-constraint computation, standard NSGA-II, full SLSQP refinement, and surrogate-guided refinement over ten matched seeds on four data-informed synthetic benchmarks. Exact computation increased from 51.25 seconds on the two-lot instance to 9.52 hours on the largest completed instance, with most intermediate SCIP points on larger instances ending at the time limit. Across the evaluated methods, surrogate gating reduced refinement calls by 75\% and lowered mean runtime from 1,478.48 to 410.52 seconds on the eight-lot instance. Full refinement led median hypervolume on three instances, but Holm-adjusted paired evidence supported its advantage over standard NSGA-II only on I02; on I04, both exceeded the selective variant. The evidence shows that mathematical intensification should be allocated according to planning scale, certification needs, and the Pareto-quality loss decision makers are prepared to accept.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Production planning must frequently commit heterogeneous resources before uncertain economic conditions become observable, while technological or biological lead times make many assignments expensive to reverse. Agricultural planning presents this challenge in a particularly transparent form because land, planting windows, maturation periods, recovery requirements, and correlated output prices jointly determine which production sequences remain feasible and economically defensible \citep{Ahumada2009,Tan2012}.

The resulting planning problem cannot be reduced to continuous resource utilization, because deliberately unoccupied capacity can emerge for fundamentally different operational reasons. Some inactivity is mandatory when calendars, maturation, setup, crop-family recovery, or horizon boundaries prevent production, whereas other inactivity is endogenous when a planner postpones an event, leaves a slot unused, or reduces cultivated area to improve the expected-revenue--risk compromise. Production research has long shown that zero-production periods and inserted idle time can be economically or operationally rational rather than automatically inefficient \citep{WagnerWhitin1958,KanetSridharan2000,Jodlbauer2006,TrevinoMartinez2022}.

Agricultural models nevertheless add empirical and structural complications that classical lot-sizing or machine-scheduling abstractions do not represent directly, including geographically variable yields and location-specific crop eligibility. Harvest timing also follows crop-specific maturation, while prices move jointly across markets and thereby connect otherwise separate production decisions through correlated economic exposure. Existing studies address uncertain maturation, demand, harvest, and yield, while portfolio formulations connect crop diversification with financial or environmental exposure \citep{Tan2012,Radulescu2014,Burbano2022}; however, implementable weekly plans must also coordinate discrete event sequences with continuous cultivated areas.

This mixed discrete--continuous structure requires choosing among exact optimization, evolutionary exploration, and matheuristic refinement according to their distinct computational and evidentiary roles. Exact multiobjective procedures offer status-bearing incumbent trade-offs, population-based search explores extensive nonconvex schedule spaces, and conditional mathematical refinement can improve continuous allocations after discrete crop identities and dates have been decoded \citep{Mavrotas2009,Deb2002,Hu2024}. Refining every offspring can nevertheless become computationally dominant, which motivates surrogate-guided allocation of optimization effort rather than surrogate replacement of the economic objectives \citep{Jin2011,Liu2024}.

Accordingly, this study examines three questions that connect production-planning value, computational scale, and the selective allocation of mathematical improvement within a common evolutionary framework. RQ1 examines how rapidly exact $\varepsilon$-constraint computation becomes costly as farms, lots, crops, planning weeks, and feasible events increase jointly; RQ2 compares standard NSGA-II with exhaustive and selective refinement under a pooled empirical Pareto reference, while retaining the status-aware SCIP archive as a sensitivity benchmark; and RQ3 evaluates whether surrogate-guided selection reduces refinement effort while retaining defensible Pareto quality under constrained runtime budgets.

The study contributes a weekly bi-objective production-planning model that maximizes expected gross revenue and minimizes covariance-based monetary price risk under empirical eligibility and agronomic feasibility, together with a controlled comparison of four computational roles. The experiment reports certification status, Pareto quality, runtime, refinement effort, and ex-post operational diagnostics without treating utilization as an optimization objective, without equating every inactive period with inefficiency, and without extending agricultural evidence into unsupported cross-industry empirical claims.

\section{Related Research}

\subsection{Production planning under uncertainty}

Production planning under uncertainty requires explicit trade-offs among economic performance, exposure, timing, and heterogeneous resource availability, because a single utilization measure cannot distinguish productive discipline from strategically preserved flexibility. Reviews of modern planning problems document expanding operational constraints and solution families, while hybridization research shows why structured problems increasingly combine mathematical programming with metaheuristic exploration \citep{Battaia2013,Boysen2022,Battaia2022}.

Classical production research also establishes that inactivity cannot be interpreted uniformly as waste, because zero-production periods may support economically or operationally preferable schedules. The Wagner--Whitin lot-sizing model admits zero-production periods when setup and inventory economics favour temporally concentrated production \citep{WagnerWhitin1958}, while the scheduling taxonomy of \citet{KanetSridharan2000} identifies inserted idle time as a purposeful decision under several performance criteria. Integrated lot-sizing and scheduling further connects production quantities, setups, and sequencing decisions \citep{Jodlbauer2006}, demonstrating a structural principle that transfers across sectors even when its empirical magnitude remains application-specific.

Contemporary energy-aware scheduling reinforces that distinction because planned idle time can reduce energy cost or emissions when tariffs and job sequences vary temporally. \citet{TrevinoMartinez2022} explicitly show that strategically planned idle time need not constitute waste, while maintenance-integrated scheduling uses inserted inactivity to exploit time-of-use tariffs \citep{An2023}. These studies support a cautious conceptual claim that inactivity may be purposeful, rather than an empirical claim that manufacturing results automatically generalize to agriculture.

Agricultural production planning combines that general structural distinction with biological timing, uncertain market outcomes, spatially variable productivity, and operational decisions extending from planting through harvest. The agri-food review by \citet{Ahumada2009} connects planning across these decisions, while \citet{Tan2012} represent demand, maturation, harvest, and yield risk for annual crops. Mandatory inactivity therefore arises whenever sowing calendars, maturation, family recovery, or horizon feasibility preclude another event, whereas endogenous inactivity arises from decoded waiting choices, unused event slots, and reduced cultivated areas.

Portfolio formulations provide a complementary economic perspective because crop returns are jointly distributed, meaning diversification depends on covariance rather than independent crop-specific penalties. In related applications, \citet{Radulescu2014} integrate expected return with financial and environmental risks, whereas \citet{Burbano2022} apply decision analysis and portfolio theory to horticultural diversification. The present setting connects that covariance-aware perspective with weekly lot occupation, continuous cultivated area, and empirical municipality--crop eligibility, while deliberately excluding weather, yield, behavioural, and complete supply-chain uncertainty.

\subsection{Multiobjective methods in agricultural planning}

Agricultural multiobjective studies increasingly connect economic performance with environmental exposure, distribution, sustainability, and interactive decision support while preserving application-specific agronomic restrictions. Recent Colombian cultivation research integrates production and distribution decisions \citep{Vergara2025}, enhanced NSGA-II designs incorporate fuzzy information and expert rules \citep{Huang2025}, and interactive evolutionary optimization reduces evaluation demands while supporting agricultural trade-off exploration \citep{Kropp2026}.

Matheuristics exploit complementary strengths by assigning combinatorial exploration to a heuristic while mathematical programming resolves a structured subproblem or improves selected candidate solutions. In multiobjective cellular manufacturing, \citet{Hu2024} embed mathematical optimization within evolutionary scheduling, showing how exact structure can support rather than replace population-based search. This decomposition is especially relevant when fixing discrete identities and times converts the remaining allocation problem into a more tractable continuous program.

Production applications demonstrate several ways to connect mathematical and heuristic components, including formulation-driven neighborhoods, decomposition, route improvement, and coordinated scheduling decisions. \citet{Li2022} combine an efficient formulation with a matheuristic for unrelated parallel machines, while \citet{Qin2024} address capacitated multi-level lot sizing with substitution and backorders. Hybrid evolutionary mechanisms also coordinate parallel batching with delivery decisions \citep{Kucukkoc2024}, while related designs improve production routing \citep{Vadseth2023} and integrate production with distribution scheduling \citep{Vie2025}, confirming that structured hybrids can broaden computational reach across planning levels.

Industrial evidence also indicates that practical matheuristics must be judged through computational reliability and decision quality under realistic planning horizons rather than through algorithm labels alone. \citet{Seyfi2026} integrate lot sizing, machine scheduling, shifts, and workforce planning for washing-machine production, obtaining scalable feasible solutions when longer horizons challenge direct optimization. These studies motivate conditional mathematical refinement, but they rarely isolate whether refining every evolutionary candidate is preferable to allocating refinement through learned utility.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted optimization}

Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation replaces or prioritizes expensive evaluations using models trained on previously evaluated candidates, thereby treating computation as a resource-allocation decision. Early synthesis emphasizes that imperfect surrogates require explicit model management and continued contact with true evaluations to avoid misleading the search \citep{Jin2011}. Multiobjective surveys similarly identify accuracy, dimensionality, infill selection, and limited evaluation budgets as central design challenges for computationally expensive optimization \citep{Chugh2019}.

More recent reviews broaden this foundation by classifying surrogate models, algorithmic roles, applications, and unresolved questions across continuous and combinatorial expensive optimization. \citet{He2023} review surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms across several problem classes and applications, while \citet{Liu2024} show that expensive combinatorial settings remain substantially less developed than continuous counterparts. A recent differential-evolution review likewise stresses that real-world value depends on whether surrogate overhead remains small relative to the evaluations being rationed \citep{Yu2025}.

Current algorithmic developments improve relationship learning and local-region management for expensive multiobjective problems, but their customary goal remains prediction or replacement of expensive objective evaluations. \citet{Pierce2025} learn informative comparison relationships among candidate solutions, whereas \citet{Wang2026} adapt local-region search for challenging high-dimensional expensive multiobjective optimization problems within demanding practical settings. The present study instead predicts realized optimization gain and uses that estimate only to decide which candidates receive a true continuous-refinement call.

Taken together, these streams establish the components required for the present comparison but leave limited controlled evidence about how continuous-refinement effort should be allocated inside a common evolutionary backbone for weekly agricultural schedules. The study therefore makes a focused comparative contribution by holding representation, initialization, evolutionary operators, and evaluation budgets constant while varying refinement allocation, without assigning individual novelty to established algorithmic or agricultural components.

\section{Materials and Data}

\subsection{Planning context and productivity data}

The decision setting uses data-informed synthetic Colombian farm instances divided into heterogeneous lots, rather than observations of specific operating farms, with each production event assigning a crop, sowing week, harvest week, and cultivated area to one lot. The source productivity table contains 17,059 municipality--crop--planting-month observations across 17 municipalities and 40 recorded agricultural products, providing the evidence used to parameterize crop eligibility and yields. The completed synthetic benchmarks use empirically supported subsets containing between 5 and 12 crops, rather than incorporating all 40 products in every instance.

Benchmark scale was informed by an earlier empirical characterization of small-scale agricultural production in Santander using the DANE Third National Agricultural Census. In that study, \citet{Talero2018} examined Guanent\'a's 17 municipalities, identified 14,764 monoculture lots, and retained 7,039 open-field lots not exceeding 5 ha and having documented historical yields. The present study uses this evidence to define the small-farm scale of the benchmark design; the individual farms and lot partitions analysed here are nevertheless synthetic constructions designed for controlled computational comparison. The empirical design therefore draws on an earlier regional characterization, whereas the optimization formulation and computational experiment reported here are defined independently for the present study.

The earlier regional analysis also documented substantial sparsity and heterogeneity across municipality--product observations, motivating explicit location-specific yield information rather than homogeneous productivity across all lots. The present procedure implements that principle independently: current productivity evidence determines municipality--crop support, crop eligibility requires at least three observations for a lot's municipality--crop pair, and expected yields are estimated hierarchically from current crop, municipality, and planting-period evidence. If no crop reaches the support threshold for a synthetic lot, the crop with the greatest local support is retained to avoid a structurally unusable lot. Feasible event variables are created only when the empirical eligibility rule is satisfied, the implied sowing week is admissible, harvest occurs within the horizon, and expected yield is positive.

\subsection{Prices and uncertainty}

Wholesale price observations used in this paper were retrieved directly from the official DANE Sistema de Informaci\'on de Precios y Abastecimiento del Sector Agropecuario (SIPSA) historical wholesale-price publications \citep{DANESIPSA}. The analysis focuses on Bucaramanga's Centroabastos market, for which the annual workbooks covering 2018--2026 were harmonized into consistent weekly product-level price series. Workbook fields are normalized to common date, product, source, and price definitions; observations are filtered to Centroabastos, duplicates are removed, and weekly prices are averaged, with source files and hashes retained in the input provenance. Use of SIPSA as the regional wholesale-price source is consistent with the earlier Santander planning study \citep{Talero2018}, although every price series and forecast used in the present experiment was reconstructed from the updated official SIPSA source.

Short internal gaps of at most four weeks are linearly interpolated, after which unresolved leading or trailing values are propagated from the nearest observation; any remaining nonpositive or missing values halt preprocessing. Model selection uses the final 52 observed weeks as one terminal holdout, rather than a rolling-origin design, and compares ARIMA, Gaussian-process regression, and an ARIMA-plus-Gaussian-process residual hybrid separately for each product. ARIMA candidates are estimated on log prices over $p,q\in\{0,1\}$ and $d\in\{0,1\}$, with the training-series specification selected by AIC; the Gaussian process uses lagged log prices, annual Fourier terms, and a Mat\'ern kernel, while the hybrid models residual structure only when at least 30 residual observations are available. The product-level model minimizing holdout sMAPE is refitted to the complete series and generates the 104-week path, whose first 52 weeks serve shorter instances. The selected forecasting model and its terminal holdout error for every product are reported in Table~\ref{tab:forecast}, allowing direct comparison across the retained series.

A Ledoit--Wolf covariance estimate of crop log returns supplies a positive-semidefinite dependence matrix, with numerical symmetrization and a diagonal adjustment only if required for convexity. This representation captures contemporaneous cross-crop price dependence within each week, but it does not model cross-period covariance, weather, yield, demand, or behavioural uncertainty.

All optimization calculations are performed in Colombian pesos (COP), whereas publication tables and figures apply a frozen reporting rate of 3,306.86 COP/USD dated 28 September 2026. Accordingly, when $p_{kt}$ is expressed in COP/kg, its reported dollar value is obtained as $p^{\mathrm{USD}}_{kt}=p_{kt}/3306.86$ without altering Pareto dominance in any comparison. Gross revenue in million COP is converted to thousand USD by multiplication by $1000/3306.86$, while risk based on million-COP exposure is converted by $(1000/3306.86)^2$. Positive constant rescaling leaves Pareto dominance unchanged, although the quadratic risk unit changes by the square of the monetary conversion factor.

Because a reproducible market-throughput series was unavailable for the reported experiment, the market-capacity constraint was not activated and the analysis does not use SIPSA-A demand information.

\subsection{Benchmark instances}

Each data-informed synthetic benchmark specifies a total coordinated area, which is allocated reproducibly among farms while constraining every individual farm to remain within the 5-ha small-farm envelope. Each farm's area is then partitioned among heterogeneous lots, and municipalities are assigned from the current productivity evidence; local empirical support subsequently determines crop eligibility and yield tensors. Network-level benchmark area can exceed 5 ha because it aggregates several small farms, so the small-scale restriction applies to the individual production unit rather than to the coordinated network. The benchmark ladder increases through farm count, lot count, total coordinated area, crop count, and planning horizon under fixed instance seeds. Table~\ref{tab:instances} reports all benchmark instances satisfying the predefined completeness criterion and summarizes their main dimensions for the subsequent computational comparison.

An instance entered the comparative analysis only when one exact run and all ten prescribed seeds (2026--2035) for each stochastic method had completed successfully. The inclusion check required successful status and an available nondominated approximation for every prescribed run, without admitting partial method--seed combinations. Application of this predefined rule retained \NIncludedInstances{} complete instances for analysis, among which the largest computationally evaluated benchmark contains \LargestIncludedLots{} heterogeneous lots.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Model assumptions and notation}

The model considers farms partitioned into lots over a finite weekly horizon, during which the planner selects crops, harvest dates, and cultivated areas for feasible production events. Feasibility reflects empirical crop eligibility, planting calendars, crop-specific maturation, exclusive lot occupation, and botanical-family recovery requirements, while schedule quality depends on expected gross revenue and correlated monetary price risk. Continuous land occupation is not imposed because inactive periods may result either from agronomic restrictions or from the trade-off between expected revenue and price risk.

\noindent\textit{Modelling assumptions and boundaries.}
Planning periods are weekly, crop maturity is deterministic within each benchmark, and every selected event is harvested after its corresponding maturation time has elapsed. Yields are conditional expectations based on lot, crop, and sowing information, whereas expected prices are forecasts available when the production plan is constructed. Dependence among crop prices is represented through contemporaneous covariance, while weather, yield, behavioural, demand, and cross-period price uncertainty remain outside the present risk measure. Production and harvesting costs are excluded, which means that $F_1$ measures expected gross revenue rather than profit, and land utilization is evaluated after optimization. Zero production remains a mathematically feasible boundary solution, although its inclusion does not imply that the model recommends inactivity under ordinary operating conditions.

\subsection{Bi-objective model}

The objectives are
\begin{align}
\max\quad F_1 &= \sum_{k\in\Kset}\sum_{t\in\Tset}e_{kt}, \label{eq:revenue}\\
\min\quad F_2 &= \sum_{t\in\Tset}\mathbf e_t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf e_t, \label{eq:risk}
\end{align}
subject to
\begin{align}
x_{kt} &= \sum_{l:(l,k,t)\in\Eset} r_{lk,s(k,t)}z_{lkt}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset, \label{eq:production}\\
e_{kt} &= \frac{p_{kt}x_{kt}}{10^6}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset, \label{eq:exposure}\\
0&\le z_{lkt}\le A_l y_{lkt}, && (l,k,t)\in\Eset, \label{eq:link}\\
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal C_{lw}}y_{lkt}&\le1, && l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset, \label{eq:occupancy}\\
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal C^q_{lw}}y_{lkt}&\le1, && l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset,\ q\in\Qset, \label{eq:rotation}\\
y_{lkt}&\in\{0,1\},\ z_{lkt}\ge0, && (l,k,t)\in\Eset, \nonumber\\
x_{kt},e_{kt}&\ge0, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset. \nonumber
\end{align}
With $\mathbf e_t=(e_{kt})_{k\in\Kset}$, equations~\eqref{eq:production} and \eqref{eq:exposure} aggregate production and monetary exposure, equation~\eqref{eq:link} couples event activation with physical lot area, and equations~\eqref{eq:occupancy} and \eqref{eq:rotation} impose occupation and family-recovery conflicts. The first objective represents expected gross sales revenue rather than net profit because production and harvesting costs remain outside the present formulation. The second objective aggregates contemporaneous crop-price covariance by harvest week without representing serial price dependence, while neither objective directly rewards land occupation. Consequently, lower exposure may result from smaller cultivated areas, fewer production events, or discretionary inactivity within otherwise feasible weekly production schedules.

\noindent\textit{Market capacity.}
When independent observations of weekly market capacity are available, sales from each product group can be limited through the following constraint:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k:g(k)=g}x_{kt}\le D_{gt},\qquad g\in\Gset,\ t\in\Tset. \label{eq:optional-demand}
\end{equation}
Because the available SIPSA records describe wholesale prices rather than independently measured purchasing capacity, this restriction was omitted from the computational experiments reported here.

\subsection{Reference solutions}

The reference procedure begins by determining the maximum expected gross revenue attainable within the base feasible region defined by the model:
\begin{equation}
I^{\max}=\max\{F_1:(x,y,z,e)\in\Omega\}, \label{eq:maxrevenue}
\end{equation}
Here, $\Omega$ denotes the feasible region formed by the base constraints and variable domains introduced in Section~4.2 of the mathematical formulation.
For each prescribed $\eta\in\{0,0.1,\ldots,1\}$, SCIP then solves
\begin{align}
\min\quad &\rho,\nonumber\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\rho\ge F_2, \label{eq:epigraph}\\
&F_1\ge\eta I^{\max}, \label{eq:epsilon}\\
&(x,y,z,e)\in\Omega.\nonumber
\end{align}
When a feasible incumbent is found, an optional strong-Pareto tie break maximizes $F_1$ subject to the attained risk plus a numerical tolerance. Solver status and relative gap are retained for every point, so a feasible time-limited incumbent is not relabelled as certified optimal.

Because the resulting optimization problem combines discrete event choices with continuous cultivated areas, the next section introduces evolutionary search and mathematical refinement strategies suited to this structure.

\subsection{Solution representation and decoding}

The decoder enforces empirical eligibility, temporal availability, botanical-family recovery, horizon feasibility, and positive output before evaluating gross revenue and risk. Mandatory inactivity arises from sowing calendars, maturation, setup, family recovery, and horizon truncation, whereas endogenous inactivity arises from unused slots, waiting genes permitting zero to four additional weeks, and reduced cultivated-area fractions. Distinguishing these two sources of inactivity prevents every unoccupied interval from being interpreted automatically as direct evidence of scheduling inefficiency.

\subsection{NSGA-II and area refinement}

Binary tournament selection, crossover probability 0.90, mutation, nondominated sorting, and crowding-based environmental selection operate with a population of 80 candidates over 150 generations. Ten fixed seeds permit stochastic replication without changing any instance data, while every candidate retains true decoded or mathematically refined economic objective values. All three evolutionary variants call one shared initialization routine, consume the same random draws for a matched seed, and retain only the natural zero-production boundary schedule as a deterministic extreme; no method receives an additional hand-crafted high-production chromosome.

\noindent\textit{Baseline NSGA-II.}
Standard NSGA-II retains the decoded cultivated areas and objective values without invoking continuous mathematical refinement, thereby providing the common low-cost evolutionary reference for both hybrid variants.

\noindent\textit{Full refinement.}

Conditional on a decoded discrete schedule, area allocation has a linear gross-revenue expression, a positive-semidefinite quadratic risk expression, physical lot constraints, and fixed event timing. In the NSGA-II+refinement method, every offspring is submitted to this continuous subproblem before environmental selection uses its resulting objective values. Although the subproblem has convex quadratic structure, the reported computational implementation solves the continuous refinement with SLSQP rather than SCIP.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted refinement}

H-NSGA-II uses the same evolutionary operators and SLSQP refinement as the full-refinement method, but limits mathematical refinement to 25\% of the offspring produced during each generation. Candidate refinements are sampled during the first five generations, thereby providing initial observations of realized improvement before the surrogate begins ranking new candidates. After at least 30 realized gains are available, an Extra Trees regressor ranks candidates, while 20\% of each refinement budget remains reserved for random exploration. Selection, nondominated sorting, and reported objectives always use decoded or SLSQP-refined values, because surrogate predictions determine only which candidates receive refinement.

Figure~\ref{fig:method_variants} isolates the principal algorithmic contrast by holding evolutionary generation and Pareto survival constant while changing only how conditional area refinement is allocated. The surrogate estimates realized refinement gain for candidate prioritization, whereas every selection decision and archived objective value remains grounded in decoded or SLSQP-refined evaluations.

\subsection{Computational experiment}

The computational comparison combined SCIP incumbent archives with three stochastic methods evaluated under identical benchmark data and matched initialization conditions. In total, each included instance comprised one exact run and 30 stochastic runs, corresponding to ten matched seeds for every evolutionary method. Standard NSGA-II used the same random initialization protocol as both refinement variants; archived runs with privileged deterministic initialization were excluded from the primary comparison. An instance entered tables and inferential comparisons only when every required method--seed result was available, ensuring that no partial larger-instance evidence affected method rankings.

SCIP solves the mixed-integer quadratically constrained formulation at 11 gross-revenue thresholds for I01--I04, using four threads and a target relative gap of $10^{-4}$. Instance-specific limits are 300, 600, 1,200, and 1,800 s per solve, while the optional strong-Pareto tie break can require a second solve and therefore raise total runtime beyond 11 times the nominal point limit.

\subsection{Performance measures}

For each complete instance, the primary reference is the nondominated union of every finite, duplicate-free point produced by SCIP and all thirty stochastic runs. The ideal and componentwise spans of this pooled empirical front define normalization, while hypervolume uses reference point $(1.1,1.1)$ in normalized minimization space; higher hypervolume and lower IGD$^+$ or additive epsilon are preferred. A secondary analysis repeats every indicator against the nondominated SCIP incumbent archive, whose time-limited points may have nonzero or uninformative gaps, so the primary analysis no longer assigns SCIP zero distance merely because it supplied the reference.

Additional measures include nondominated point count, total runtime, refinement calls, cumulative refinement time, and useful-refinement hit rate for every applicable method and instance. Medians summarize stochastic quality, runtime is additionally reported through its interquartile range and mean where relevant, and matched seeds support paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank comparisons. Three method contrasts are evaluated within each instance and metric, Holm correction controls their familywise error rate, rank-biserial correlation describes direction and magnitude, and paired bootstrap intervals quantify uncertainty around median differences.

Operational diagnostics are calculated after optimization from decoded schedules, meaning that they serve as interpretive measures rather than optimization objectives or binding constraints. For a decoded event $e$ with cultivated area $a_e$, sowing week $s_e$, and harvest week $h_e$, area-adjusted space--time utilization is
\begin{equation}
U=\frac{\sum_e a_e(h_e-s_e)}{|\Tset|\sum_{l\in\Lset}A_l}. \label{eq:utilization}
\end{equation}
Lot temporal occupancy is $U_l^{\mathrm{time}}=|\cup_{e\in\mathcal E_l}[s_e,h_e)|/|\Tset|$, while mean idle weeks averages the horizon weeks outside those occupied unions across lots. Together, these post-hoc measures distinguish partial-area intensity from temporal occupation, although they cannot by themselves classify every inactive interval as mandatory or economically chosen.

\subsection{Refinement and resulting schedules}

\section{Discussion}

Matching initialization materially changes the comparative account because the archived baseline had received a deterministic high-production chromosome that neither hybrid variant received. Under the new pooled scaling, replacing that privileged initialization with the shared random initializer lowers baseline median hypervolume from 0.864 to 0.862 on I01, from 1.007 to 0.971 on I02, from 0.831 to 0.746 on I03, and from 0.831 to 0.811 on I04. Full refinement consequently becomes the descriptive median leader on three instances rather than one, although paired evidence supports its advantage over the corrected baseline only on I02 and therefore does not justify a general superiority claim.

The pooled empirical reference removes automatic zero-distance scores for the SCIP method and acknowledges that most intermediate SCIP points on I02--I04 are time-limited incumbents rather than certified efficient solutions. Retaining the former SCIP-incumbent reference as a sensitivity benchmark preserves every stochastic hypervolume rank, which makes the revised ordering robust to that reference choice while leaving absolute distance indicators reference-dependent. SCIP therefore supplies status, gaps, endpoints, and tractability evidence, whereas the pooled union supplies the more defensible primary geometry for comparative indicators.

Continuous area refinement can improve a decoded candidate locally without guaranteeing a better final archive because accepted changes alter parent selection, crowding, and subsequent genetic trajectories while leaving the discrete schedule neighborhood unchanged. Surrogate gating treats mathematical intensification as a computational budget by predicting realized refinement gain, not by approximating economic objectives or determining archive membership; its one-quarter call allocation increases useful-call rates and reduces refinement time, although the I04 paired results show a measurable final-front cost relative to both alternatives.

The production schedules also demonstrate why inactive periods cannot be interpreted automatically as inefficiency without first examining their agronomic and economic causes. Agronomic calendars, maturation, setup, botanical-family recovery, and horizon boundaries create mandatory gaps, whereas waiting genes, unused slots, and cultivated-area reductions create endogenous gaps that may alter harvest prices or monetary exposure. Because utilization is neither an objective nor a constraint, the reported space--time utilization, lot temporal occupancy, and mean idle weeks remain post-hoc diagnostics whose interpretation requires the decoded schedule and cannot be reduced to a universal efficiency judgment.

The cross-industry literature supports a structural rather than empirical transferability claim concerning the conditions under which planned inactivity can remain operationally rational. Lot-sizing models, inserted-idle-time scheduling, integrated production planning, and energy-aware scheduling all show that zero production or planned inactivity can be rational under specific costs, constraints, and temporal incentives \citep{WagnerWhitin1958,KanetSridharan2000,Jodlbauer2006,TrevinoMartinez2022,An2023}. Agricultural production differs in its biological calendars, spatial yield evidence, and crop-family recovery, so those manufacturing findings justify distinguishing forms of inactivity without establishing that their estimated effects or preferred policies apply directly to farms.

Several limitations bound inference from the controlled experiment because four complete data-informed synthetic instances and ten stochastic seeds per method cannot support universal algorithm rankings. The pooled reference is an empirical nondominated union rather than a proven Pareto set, while time-limited SCIP points restrict certification beyond I01 and the single terminal forecasting holdout provides less temporal validation than a rolling-origin design. The covariance objective captures cross-crop exposure within each harvest week but excludes cross-period covariance, weather, yield, behavioural, and demand uncertainty; the optional market-throughput constraint remained inactive because no reproducible market-throughput series was available. Future work should compare equal wall-clock budgets, vary surrogate allocation rules, complete larger benchmark ladders, strengthen temporal forecast validation, and test explicitly modelled production-intensity requirements before claiming robustness under stronger utilization expectations.

\section{Conclusion}

This study formulates weekly agricultural production planning as a bi-objective event model combining discrete crop calendars, continuous cultivated area, empirical crop eligibility, and contemporaneous covariance-based monetary price risk. The formulation preserves expected gross-revenue maximization and correlated price-risk minimization as its only objectives, while operational utilization remains an explicitly post-hoc diagnostic rather than an optimization target.

The computational evidence reveals a three-way trade-off among certification, Pareto quality, and response time that becomes increasingly consequential as instance scale increases. Exact $\varepsilon$-constraint computation provides status-bearing incumbents but grows from seconds to hours and loses full-front certification as scale increases, whereas full refinement records the highest stochastic median hypervolume on three instances after initialization is matched. Paired evidence supports only selected contrasts, and surrogate gating reduces refinement calls by 75\% while concentrating useful calls, although its computational savings coincide with lower Pareto quality on I04.

The operational interpretation remains consequential because unoccupied time combines mandatory agronomic restrictions with endogenous economic choices and therefore cannot be labelled inefficient without examining the decoded schedule. Empirical generalization is limited to the evaluated data-informed synthetic agricultural benchmarks, but the underlying architecture may transfer structurally to production-planning systems where discrete sequencing, continuous allocation, time-dependent economic exposure, optional inactivity, and costly mathematical intensification must be coordinated under one computational budget.
