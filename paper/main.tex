\title{Agricultural Production Planning under Correlated Price Risk: Exact Reference Computation and Surrogate-Guided Continuous Refinement}

\author{\name{Leonardo Hern\'an Talero-Sarmiento}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agricultural production planning couples discrete crop calendars with continuous land allocation under uncertain output prices. This study formulates a bi-objective mixed-integer planning model that maximizes expected income and minimizes covariance-based price risk while enforcing empirical crop eligibility, lot occupancy, maturation, and botanical-family recovery. Four publication-complete instances cover one or two farms, 2--8 lots, 5--12 crops, 52--104 planning weeks, and 0.6--6.0 ha. Exact $\varepsilon$-constraint runs in SCIP provide an incumbent reference, standard NSGA-II supplies a low-cost baseline, and two matheuristics apply either full or surrogate-guided continuous quadratic area refinement with SLSQP. Each stochastic method is evaluated over ten seeds using normalized hypervolume, IGD$^+$, additive epsilon, nondominated-set size, runtime, and refinement effort. Exact-run time rises from 51.25 s on the two-lot instance to 9.52 h on the largest completed instance, while most intermediate SCIP points on the larger instances terminate at the time limit. Surrogate gating reduces refinement calls from 12,000 to 3,000 per replication. On the 8-lot, 104-week instance, it reduces mean total runtime from 1,478.48 to 410.52 s relative to full refinement, but median hypervolume also declines from 0.745 to 0.718. Standard NSGA-II records the highest median hypervolume among the stochastic methods on three of four instances. The evidence therefore supports method selection as a trade-off among reference quality, computation, and planning scale, rather than a universal algorithm ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Crop planning commits scarce land and operational time before harvest prices become observable, while maturation delays and recovery requirements make many decisions costly to reverse. These commitments affect farm income, exposure to correlated market movements, and the productive continuity of heterogeneous lots, making planning consequential for both livelihoods and resource stewardship. Agri-food planning reviews accordingly emphasize that useful decision models must connect biological calendars, market conditions, and operational restrictions within a coherent temporal representation \citep{Ahumada2009}.

Production planning more generally has moved beyond deterministic cost minimization toward settings involving uncertain parameters, heterogeneous resources, interdependent decisions, and several competing objectives. Exact multiobjective methods can provide status-bearing trade-offs, whereas evolutionary methods can explore extensive nonconvex decision spaces and return diverse alternatives within a single run \citep{Mavrotas2009,Deb2002}. However, growing combinations of products, resources, and periods make computational scalability inseparable from solution quality, particularly when mathematical subproblems are repeatedly embedded inside population-based search.

Agriculture intensifies these difficulties because planting and harvest dates are biologically linked, yield evidence varies geographically, crop families require recovery intervals, and prices co-move across markets. Existing models address uncertain maturation, demand, harvest, and yield \citep{Tan2012}, while portfolio-based farm planning connects diversification with financial and environmental exposure \citep{Radulescu2014,Burbano2022}. Nevertheless, an implementable plan must integrate those economic trade-offs with weekly crop sequences and continuous land allocation rather than treating diversification as an aggregate acreage decision.

The resulting model contains a combinatorial scheduling layer and a structured continuous allocation layer, creating a natural opportunity for exact optimization, evolutionary search, and matheuristic decomposition. Production research shows that hybrid methods can preserve useful mathematical structure while extending practical scale \citep{Battaia2022,Hu2024}, yet continuous refinement becomes burdensome when every evolutionary offspring receives an optimization call. Surrogate assistance can allocate expensive evaluations more selectively, although savings remain meaningful only when measured alongside changes in Pareto-front quality \citep{Jin2011,Liu2024}.

This study therefore addresses three questions that connect planning value, computational tractability, and the consequences of selectively allocating mathematical refinement across candidate agricultural schedules. RQ1 asks how rapidly exact $\varepsilon$-constraint reference computation becomes costly as farms, lots, crops, planning weeks, and feasible production events increase jointly. RQ2 asks how standard NSGA-II and two refinement-based matheuristics compare against status-aware SCIP incumbent archives using replicated Pareto-quality and computational-effort indicators. RQ3 asks whether surrogate-guided selection can reduce continuous-refinement effort while preserving enough frontier quality to offer a defensible alternative under constrained runtime budgets.

To answer these questions, the study formulates a weekly bi-objective event model maximizing expected income while minimizing covariance-based monetary price risk under empirical eligibility and agronomic constraints. The computational design compares SCIP $\varepsilon$-constraint search, standard NSGA-II, exhaustive continuous refinement, and surrogate-guided selective refinement using identical benchmark data and replicated stochastic seeds. Its contribution is a controlled, status-aware comparison that exposes certification, frontier quality, refinement calls, and runtime without claiming universal algorithmic superiority beyond the evaluated instances.

\section{Related Research}

\subsection{Production planning under uncertainty, heterogeneous resources, and multiple objectives}

Contemporary production planning increasingly represents multiple performance criteria, resource heterogeneity, and uncertainty instead of relying exclusively on deterministic efficiency or cost objectives. Line-balancing reviews document expanding model variants, operational constraints, data requirements, and exact or heuristic solution families across increasingly heterogeneous industrial production environments \citep{Battaia2013,Boysen2022}. Hybridization research further shows that increasingly structured production problems motivate combinations of mathematical programming, decomposition, and metaheuristics rather than one universally dominant solution paradigm \citep{Battaia2022}.

Multiobjective production models require explicit treatment of trade-offs because improving one operational or economic criterion can degrade another criterion without yielding a single unequivocal optimum. The $\varepsilon$-constraint method produces interpretable alternatives by optimizing one objective while systematically bounding another, although its repeated mathematical programs can become computationally demanding \citep{Mavrotas2009}. Population-based alternatives such as NSGA-II instead approximate several nondominated solutions simultaneously through elitist sorting and diversity preservation, making replicated evaluation essential for stochastic comparisons \citep{Deb2002}.

Heterogeneous resources add more than scale because suitability, productivity, capacity, and timing determine which assignments are feasible and how strongly they contribute to each objective. Parallel-batch scheduling research illustrates how hybrid evolutionary mechanisms can coordinate assignment, batching, and delivery structures that resist straightforward exact solution at realistic sizes \citep{Kucukkoc2024}. This production-planning stream establishes the need for scalable multiobjective methods, but it does not determine how expensive mathematical improvement should be distributed among agricultural candidates.

\subsection{Agricultural production planning under economic and biological constraints}

Agricultural production planning joins tactical resource allocation with biological timing, uncertain market outcomes, spatially variable productivity, and operational decisions extending from planting through harvest and distribution. The foundational agri-food review by \citet{Ahumada2009} identifies planning models across these linked decisions, while \citet{Tan2012} explicitly represent demand, maturation, harvest, and yield risk for annual crops. These characteristics distinguish agricultural planning from generic allocation because feasible decisions depend on biological calendars that constrain when occupied land becomes available again.

Portfolio formulations provide a complementary economic perspective because crop returns are jointly distributed, meaning diversification depends on covariance rather than independent crop-specific penalties. \citet{Radulescu2014} integrate expected return with financial and environmental risks, whereas \citet{Burbano2022} apply decision analysis and portfolio theory to diversification under horticultural uncertainty. Those studies motivate covariance-aware risk measurement, although their aggregate decision structures do not reproduce weekly lot occupancy and crop-family recovery requirements.

Recent agricultural multiobjective studies increasingly combine sustainability, distribution logistics, interactive decision support, and evolutionary search while retaining consequential application-specific agronomic constraints. \citet{Vergara2025} optimize cultivation and distribution decisions for Colombian yam systems, whereas \citet{Huang2025} incorporate fuzzy information and expert rules into an enhanced NSGA-II crop-planning framework. \citet{Kropp2026} add interactive decision making to evolutionary agricultural optimization and report competitive solution support with fewer evaluations, demonstrating growing interest in computationally economical decision interaction.

The present setting extends this stream by linking empirical municipality--crop eligibility, event-level weekly calendars, continuous cultivated areas, and correlated harvest-price exposure within one reproducible production model. Its risk term remains deliberately bounded because it represents covariance-based price exposure rather than weather, yield, behavioural, or complete supply-chain uncertainty.

\subsection{Matheuristics for multiobjective production planning}

Matheuristics exploit complementary strengths by assigning combinatorial exploration to a heuristic while mathematical programming resolves a structured subproblem or improves selected candidate solutions. In multiobjective cellular manufacturing, \citet{Hu2024} embed mathematical optimization within evolutionary scheduling, showing how exact structure can support rather than replace population-based search. This decomposition is especially relevant when fixing discrete identities and times converts the remaining allocation problem into a more tractable continuous program.

Production applications demonstrate several ways to connect mathematical and heuristic components, including formulation-driven neighborhoods, decomposition, route improvement, and coordinated scheduling decisions. \citet{Li2022} combine an efficient formulation with a matheuristic for unrelated parallel machines, while \citet{Qin2024} address capacitated multi-level lot sizing with substitution and backorders. Related designs improve production routing \citep{Vadseth2023} and integrate production with distribution scheduling \citep{Vie2025}, confirming that hybrid structure can broaden computational reach across planning levels.

Industrial evidence also indicates that practical matheuristics must be judged through computational reliability and decision quality under realistic planning horizons rather than through algorithm labels alone. \citet{Seyfi2026} integrate lot sizing, machine scheduling, shifts, and workforce planning for washing-machine production, obtaining scalable feasible solutions when longer horizons challenge direct optimization. These studies motivate conditional mathematical refinement, but they rarely isolate whether refining every evolutionary candidate is preferable to allocating refinement through learned utility.

\subsection{Surrogate-guided allocation of optimization effort}

Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation replaces or prioritizes expensive evaluations using models trained on previously evaluated candidates, thereby treating computation as a resource-allocation decision. Early synthesis emphasizes that imperfect surrogates require explicit model management and continued contact with true evaluations to avoid misleading the search \citep{Jin2011}. Multiobjective surveys similarly identify accuracy, dimensionality, infill selection, and limited evaluation budgets as central design challenges for computationally expensive optimization \citep{Chugh2019}.

More recent reviews broaden this foundation by classifying surrogate models, algorithmic roles, applications, and unresolved questions across continuous and combinatorial expensive optimization. \citet{He2023} review surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms across several problem classes and applications, while \citet{Liu2024} show that expensive combinatorial settings remain substantially less developed than continuous counterparts. A recent differential-evolution review likewise stresses that real-world value depends on whether surrogate overhead remains small relative to the evaluations being rationed \citep{Yu2025}.

Current algorithmic developments improve relationship learning and local-region management for expensive multiobjective problems, but their customary goal remains prediction or replacement of expensive objective evaluations. \citet{Pierce2025} learn informative comparison relationships among candidate solutions, whereas \citet{Wang2026} adapt local-region search for challenging high-dimensional expensive multiobjective optimization problems within demanding practical settings. The present study instead predicts realized optimization gain and uses that estimate only to decide which candidates receive a true continuous-refinement call.

Across these four streams, no reviewed study jointly provides weekly agricultural scheduling, covariance-based monetary risk, exact status-aware references, exhaustive continuous refinement, and surrogate-gated refinement within one controlled experiment. This gap supports a focused contribution concerning the allocation of optimization effort, while avoiding broader claims that the underlying algorithms or agricultural constraints are individually unprecedented.

\section{Decision Setting and Data}

\subsection{Study setting and productivity evidence}

The decision setting represents Colombian farms divided into heterogeneous lots. A production event assigns a crop, sowing week, harvest week, and cultivated area to one lot. The source productivity table contains 17,059 municipality--crop--planting-month observations, 17 municipalities, and 40 recorded agricultural products. These counts describe the source table; the completed benchmarks use supported subsets of 5--12 crops rather than all 40 products.

Yield estimation combines municipality, crop, and planting-period evidence. Crop eligibility requires at least three supporting observations for a lot's municipality--crop pair; if no crop passes that threshold for a generated lot, the crop with greatest support is retained to avoid a structurally unusable lot. Feasible event variables are created only when the sowing calendar permits the event, harvest occurs within the horizon, the empirical eligibility rule is satisfied, and expected yield is positive.

\subsection{Prices and uncertainty}

Weekly prices were constructed for Bucaramanga's Centroabastos market from DANE SIPSA wholesale workbooks for 2018--2026. The input provenance records the downloaded files and hashes. The experiment uses a 104-week price forecast generated from the processed weekly series; shorter instances use its first 52 weeks. A Ledoit--Wolf covariance estimate of crop log returns supplies a positive-semidefinite dependence matrix, with numerical symmetrization and a diagonal adjustment only if required for convexity.

The optimization is performed in Colombian pesos (COP). Publication tables and figures apply a frozen reporting rate of 3,306.86 COP/USD dated 28 September 2026. If $p_{kt}$ is COP/kg, then $p^{\mathrm{USD}}_{kt}=p_{kt}/3306.86$. Likewise, income in million COP is converted to thousand USD by multiplication by $1000/3306.86$, and risk based on million-COP exposure is converted by $(1000/3306.86)^2$. Positive constant rescaling leaves Pareto dominance unchanged; the quadratic risk unit changes by the square of the monetary conversion factor.

No \texttt{demand\_weekly.csv} file was present in the reproducible computational snapshot. Consequently, the reported experiment has no market-throughput cap and makes no use of SIPSA-A demand.

\subsection{Benchmark construction}

The benchmark ladder changes farms, lots, area, crop count, and horizon under fixed instance seeds. Only instances with all expected files were admitted: one exact run and ten seeds (2026--2035) for each stochastic method. The snapshot scanner reads each \texttt{metadata.json}, requires \texttt{status=ok}, and verifies the expected front before inclusion. It does not rely on a final run index. At the analysed snapshot, \NIncludedInstances{} instances meet this rule; the largest contains \LargestIncludedLots{} lots.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

The decision problem selects agronomically feasible crop events and their areas. Expected income is maximized while correlated price risk is minimized. The formulation is expressed in the internal monetary units used by optimization; the ex-post USD transformation above is used for manuscript reporting.

\subsection{Sets and Parameters}

Let $\Fset$ be the farms, $\Lset$ the lots, and $\Lset_f\subseteq\Lset$ the lots belonging to farm $f$. Let $\Kset$ denote crops, $\Tset$ weekly planning periods, and $\Tset_k\subseteq\Tset$ feasible harvest periods for crop $k$. For an optional market extension, $\Gset$ denotes sales groups. The sets $\mathcal{C}_{lw}$ and $\mathcal{C}^{q}_{lw}$ contain, respectively, crop--harvest events that occupy lot $l$ during week $w$ and events from botanical family $q$ whose occupation or recovery intervals conflict in that week.

Parameter $A_l$ is lot area (m$^2$); $n_k$ is crop maturation duration (weeks); $s(k,t)=t-n_k$ is the sowing week implied by harvest $t$; $r_{lks}$ is expected yield (kg/m$^2$); and $p_{kt}$ is expected price (COP/kg). The indicator $b_{lk}$ equals one when empirical evidence makes crop $k$ eligible for lot $l$. Matrix $\Sigma\succeq0$ is the covariance matrix of crop price log returns, $q(k)$ is botanical family, and $g(k)$ is sales group. The optional parameter $D_{gt}$ would cap group-$g$ throughput in week $t$, but is absent from the reported base experiment.

\subsection{Decision Variables}

For every feasible event $(l,k,t)$, define
\begin{equation}
y_{lkt}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if crop }k\text{ is activated on lot }l\text{ for harvest in week }t,\\0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and let $z_{lkt}\geq0$ be cultivated area (m$^2$). Aggregate harvested quantity is $x_{kt}\geq0$ (kg). Monetary exposure is $e_{kt}\geq0$, measured in million COP, and $\rho\geq0$ is the epigraph variable used for quadratic risk in the exact formulation.

\subsection{Bi-objective Optimization Model}

Production and exposure are aggregated through
\begin{align}
x_{kt} &= \sum_{l\in\Lset} r_{lk,s(k,t)}z_{lkt}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset_k, \label{eq:production}\\
e_{kt} &= \frac{p_{kt}x_{kt}}{10^6}, && k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset. \label{eq:exposure}
\end{align}
The two objectives are
\begin{align}
\max\quad F_1 &= \sum_{k\in\Kset}\sum_{t\in\Tset}e_{kt}, \label{eq:income}\\
\min\quad F_2 &= \sum_{t\in\Tset}\mathbf e_t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\mathbf e_t, \label{eq:risk}
\end{align}
where $F_1$ is expected income in million COP and $F_2$ is correlated price risk in (million COP)$^2$. The latter is a monetary mean--variance measure, not a production cost.

Activation uses the physical lot area rather than an arbitrary large constant:
\begin{equation}
0\le z_{lkt}\le A_l y_{lkt}, \qquad l\in\Lset,\ k\in\Kset,\ t\in\Tset_k. \label{eq:link}
\end{equation}
Weekly conflict cliques enforce exclusive lot occupation,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{lw}}y_{lkt}\le1,
\qquad l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset, \label{eq:occupancy}
\end{equation}
and family-specific cliques extend this restriction through the configured recovery interval,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{(k,t)\in\mathcal{C}^{q}_{lw}}y_{lkt}\le1,
\qquad l\in\Lset,\ w\in\Tset,\ q. \label{eq:rotation}
\end{equation}
Variables exist only if $b_{lk}=1$, $s(k,t)$ is an allowed sowing week, $t$ lies within the horizon, and $r_{lk,s(k,t)}>0$. This event generation enforces empirical eligibility and temporal feasibility without adding redundant zero variables.

\noindent\textit{Optional market-throughput extension.}
If independent market-capacity evidence is available, the implementation can add
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k:g(k)=g}x_{kt}\le D_{gt},\qquad g\in\Gset,\ t\in\Tset. \label{eq:optional-demand}
\end{equation}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:optional-demand} was inactive in every result reported here.

\subsection{Epsilon-Constraint Reference Formulation}

The reference procedure first solves
\begin{equation}
I^{\max}=\max\{F_1:(\ref{eq:production})--(\ref{eq:rotation})\}. \label{eq:maxincome}
\end{equation}
For each configured $\eta\in\{0,0.1,\ldots,1\}$, SCIP then solves
\begin{align}
\min\quad &\rho,\nonumber\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\rho\ge F_2, \label{eq:epigraph}\\
&F_1\ge\eta I^{\max}, \label{eq:epsilon}\
&(\ref{eq:production})--(\ref{eq:rotation}).\nonumber
\end{align}
When a feasible incumbent is found, an optional strong-Pareto tie break maximizes $F_1$ subject to the attained risk plus a numerical tolerance. Solver status and relative gap are retained for every point, so a feasible time-limited incumbent is not relabelled as certified optimal.

\section{Solution Methods}

\subsection{Exact epsilon-constraint SCIP}

SCIP solves the mixed-integer quadratically constrained formulation with 11 income thresholds. Instance-specific limits are 300, 600, 1,200, and 1,800 s per solve for I01--I04, four threads, and a target relative gap of $10^{-4}$. Because the strong-Pareto tie break can require a second solve, total run time can exceed 11 times the point limit.

\subsection{NSGA-II}

The baseline encodes crop sequences and waiting decisions by lot. Decoding produces feasible sow--harvest events and area values, after which true income and risk are evaluated. Binary tournament selection, crossover probability 0.90, mutation, nondominated sorting, and crowding-based environmental selection operate with population 80 for 150 generations. Ten fixed seeds permit replication without changing instance data.

\subsection{Full continuous quadratic refinement}

Conditional on a decoded discrete schedule, area allocation has a linear income expression, a positive-semidefinite quadratic risk expression, physical lot constraints, and fixed event timing. The reported NSGA-II+refinement experiment sends every offspring to this continuous subproblem. Although its mathematical structure is a convex quadratic refinement, the computational backend in this experiment is SLSQP, not SCIP.

\subsection{Surrogate-assisted H-NSGA-II}

H-NSGA-II uses the same evolutionary operators and SLSQP refinement, but limits refinement to 25\% of offspring. During five warm-up generations, candidates are sampled for refinement. After at least 30 realized gains are available, an Extra Trees regressor ranks candidates; 20\% of each refinement budget remains random exploration. All selection, nondominated sorting, and reported objectives use actual decoded or SLSQP-refined values. Surrogate predictions only control access to refinement.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Exact tractability}

Total exact-run time increases from 51.25 s for I01 to 9,185.10 s (2.55 h) for I02, 22,824.80 s (6.34 h) for I03, and 34,274.58 s (9.52 h) for I04. Status evidence changes with scale. Ten of 11 I01 points are optimal and one terminates at the target gap. I02 has four optimal and seven time-limited points. I03 and I04 each have one optimal income-endpoint and ten time-limited points. Thus, SCIP remains valuable as an incumbent reference, but the experiment does not establish full-front optimality beyond the smallest instance.

\subsection{Value of full versus surrogate-guided refinement}

Full refinement makes 12,000 SLSQP calls per replication; surrogate gating makes 3,000, a 75\% reduction. On I03, median cumulative refinement time falls from 1,346.04 to 284.46 s and useful-refinement hit rate rises from 96.0\% to 98.8\%. On I04, refinement time falls from 289.67 to 71.54 s and hit rate rises from 94.8\% to 98.9\%. The higher hit rate shows concentrated allocation, but quality must be read jointly: median hypervolume falls from 0.745 to 0.718 on I03 and from 0.829 to 0.793 on I04. Surrogate gating is therefore a computational economy with a measured quality cost in these instances.

\section{Discussion}

The findings address a narrower gap than introducing an entirely new optimization family, because their main value lies in connecting agricultural scheduling with controlled refinement allocation. Relative to agricultural multiobjective models and production matheuristics, the experiment makes the computational consequence of exhaustive versus surrogate-gated continuous improvement directly observable under identical data, operators, and seeds.

The experiment separates three computational roles. SCIP supplies economically interpretable reference points and status information, but its rapid runtime growth and the prevalence of time-limited incumbents restrict certification beyond I01. Standard NSGA-II supplies diverse fronts at low and comparatively stable cost. Conditional continuous refinement exploits convex area structure, yet more local optimization does not guarantee a better global archive because refinement changes the search trajectory and consumes time without expanding the discrete schedule space.

Surrogate-guided refinement succeeds at its immediate task: it directs one quarter as many SLSQP calls toward candidates with a higher useful-refinement rate. The larger completed instances show the practical value of that allocation through substantial runtime reductions. However, lower hypervolume and worse distance indicators relative to full refinement show that some omitted calls still matter to the final front. The defensible interpretation is therefore conditional. When runtime is constrained, selective refinement offers a tunable intermediate method; when Pareto quality relative to the incumbent reference dominates computation, this experiment gives no basis for preferring H-NSGA-II over the alternatives.

The comparison also cautions against treating local-refinement success as final-front success. Hit rates exceed 98\% for H-NSGA-II on I02--I04, yet its archive can remain worse because a locally useful area change may not improve global spread, and repeated exploitation can alter diversity. Future tests should vary the refinement fraction, exploration share, and gain target, and should compare equal wall-clock budgets rather than only equal generations.

Several limits bound inference. The experiment contains four complete instances and ten stochastic seeds per method. Exact reference archives for I02--I04 contain time-limited points, so quality indicators measure agreement with an incumbent reference, not distance to a proven Pareto set. The covariance objective captures correlated price exposure but excludes weather, yield, and behavioural uncertainty. The market-throughput extension was inactive because no reproducible demand file was present. Finally, the 17,059-row productivity source has uneven municipality--crop support; empirical eligibility reduces extrapolation but does not remove data sparsity.

\section{Conclusion}

This study formulates weekly agricultural production planning as a bi-objective event model with discrete crop calendars, continuous cultivated area, empirical crop eligibility, and correlated monetary price risk. A publication-safe experiment on four complete instances compares SCIP $\varepsilon$-constraint computation, NSGA-II, full SLSQP area refinement, and surrogate-guided selective refinement.

The evidence reveals a three-way trade-off. Exact computation provides status-bearing reference points but grows from seconds to hours and loses full-front certification as scale increases. Standard NSGA-II is the lowest-cost stochastic method and achieves the highest median hypervolume among stochastic alternatives on three instances. Surrogate gating cuts refinement calls by 75\% and substantially reduces refinement and total runtime on the larger completed instances, but the savings coincide with lower median Pareto quality than full refinement. Method choice should therefore reflect the value assigned to certification, frontier quality, and response time. Larger benchmark conclusions require completion of all prescribed seeds and must be regenerated through the same snapshot rules rather than inferred from partial runs.
